# English vs Chinese Catholic Mass Comparison

**Roman Rite — Ordinary Form (Novus Ordo)**  
Based on the *Missale Romanum* (third typical edition). Both the English (ICEL 2010/2011) and Chinese versions used in Hong Kong/Taiwan are official translations of the same Latin text. Structure is identical; differences are mainly in translation style and local practice.

## Overall Structure

| Part | English | Chinese |
|------|---------|---------|
| 1 | Introductory Rites | 進堂式 / 進堂禮 |
| 2 | Liturgy of the Word | 聖道禮儀 |
| 3 | Liturgy of the Eucharist | 聖祭禮儀 |
| 4 | Concluding Rites | 禮成式 |

## Key Dialogues and Responses

### Greeting
- **English**: The Lord be with you. → And with your spirit.
- **Chinese**: 願主與你們同在。 → 也與你的心靈同在。

(Note: The 2011 English translation restored the more literal “And with your spirit” matching Latin *Et cum spiritu tuo* and the Chinese.)

### Penitential Act (Confiteor)
Both have the same three options. The most common form is essentially parallel:
- English: “I confess to almighty God and to you, my brothers and sisters...”
- Chinese: “我向全能的天主和各位教友，承認我思、言、行為上的過失……我罪，我罪，我的重罪。”

### Gloria
Omitted during Advent and Lent in both.
- English begins: “Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to people of good will.”
- Chinese: “天主在天受光榮，主愛的人在世享平安。”

### Gospel Dialogue
- English: The Lord be with you. → And with your spirit.  
  A reading from the holy Gospel according to N. → Glory to you, O Lord.
- Chinese: 願主與你們同在。 → 也與你的心靈同在。  
  恭讀聖□□福音 → 主，願光榮歸於你。

### Preface Dialogue
| English | Chinese |
|---------|---------|
| The Lord be with you. | 願主與你們同在。 |
| And with your spirit. | 也與你的心靈同在。 |
| Lift up your hearts. | 請舉心向上。 |
| We lift them up to the Lord. | 我們全心歸向上主。 |
| Let us give thanks to the Lord our God. | 請大家感謝主、我們的天主。 |
| It is right and just. | 這是理所當然的。 |

### Sanctus
- English: “Holy, holy, holy Lord God of hosts. Heaven and earth are full of your glory. Hosanna in the highest...”
- Chinese: “聖、聖、聖，上主，萬有的天主，你的光榮充滿天地。歡呼之聲，響徹雲霄。...”

### Our Father / 天主經
Content is the same (Lord’s Prayer). English often uses the traditional “Our Father, who art in heaven...” form in the liturgy.

### Lamb of God
- English: “Lamb of God, you take away the sins of the world, have mercy on us...”
- Chinese: “除免世罪的天主羔羊，求你垂憐我們。...”

### Dismissal
- English: Go forth, the Mass is ended. (or variants) → Thanks be to God.
- Chinese: 彌撒禮成。 → 感謝天主。

## Summary of Differences

1. **Structure and order of rites**: Essentially identical.
2. **Translation philosophy**:
   - English (2011 ICEL): More literal to the Latin.
   - Chinese: Official translation focused on natural Chinese expression while preserving theological meaning.
3. **Local practice**:
   - English-speaking parishes may use more Latin chant for the Ordinary (Kyrie, Gloria, Sanctus, Agnus Dei).
   - Chinese parishes (e.g. Hong Kong) typically use fully vernacular texts and local hymnals such as *頒恩*.
4. **Options**: Both allow the same options (different forms of Penitential Act, Eucharistic Prayers I–IV and others, etc.).
